# A/B Test Analysis: did moving the gate hurt retention?

[![ci](https://github.com/VasilenData/ab-test-analysis/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/VasilenData/ab-test-analysis/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

A tested experimentation toolkit (fixed-sample tests, sequential testing, CUPED) and a full analysis of a real public A/B test: the mobile game Cookie Cats moved its first progression gate from level 30 to level 40. 90,189 players were split between the two versions.

**Result:** moving the gate to level 40 **lowered day-7 retention by 0.82 percentage points** (19.02% to 18.20%, 95% CI -1.33 to -0.31, p = 0.0016, survives Holm correction). Day-1 retention moved the same direction but is not significant (p = 0.074). **Recommendation: keep the gate at level 30.**

![Lift with 95% confidence intervals](reports/lift.png)

## What the analysis does
- **Sample-ratio-mismatch check first.** The split is 44,700 vs 45,489 (49.6% / 50.4%), chi-square p = 0.0086. That is below 0.05 but above the usual 0.001 alarm line. Worth flagging to the data owner; I did not treat the result as invalid, and I say so here.
- **Two-proportion z-test** with an absolute-lift confidence interval for both retention metrics.
- **Multiple-testing correction** (Holm) across the two retention metrics.
- **Bayesian check:** P(gate_40 better) is 0.1% for day-7 retention.
- **A/A validation:** randomly splitting the data into fake arms gives 5.0% and 5.4% false positives, close to the nominal 5% in this simulation.
- **Small-sample exact check.** `fisher_exact_test` gives a two-sided Fisher exact p-value, and `exact_type_i_error` enumerates the exact false-positive rate of any test (no simulation). With 15 players per arm the pooled z-test rejects a true null 5.5% of the time at a 30% base rate (and is conservative at 0.5% to 4.6% for other rates I tried); Fisher stays below 5% everywhere. At 300 per arm the z-test is within 1 pp of 5%. Cookie Cats is far too large for this to matter; it is a guard for small experiments.
- **Power:** with this sample the minimum detectable day-7 effect is 0.74 pp. Detecting 0.5 pp would need about 97,700 players per arm.
- **Engagement (game rounds)** compared after winsorizing at the 99th percentile (493 rounds; the raw max is 49,854, a likely bot or logging error), with a bootstrap CI (-1.30 to +0.72 rounds, no clear difference) and Mann-Whitney (p = 0.050).

## Sequential testing: looking early without lying
Peeking at a fixed-sample test inflates false positives. `src/sequential.py` implements Lan-DeMets alpha-spending boundaries (O'Brien-Fleming and Pocock, solved by numerical integration, no external library) and a normal-mixture mSPRT approximation.

![Sequential testing](reports/sequential.png)

Simulation, 10 looks, 20,000 players per arm at most, baseline 19%, 4,000 runs (`python -m src.simulate`):

| Rule | False positives, no effect | Power, true effect +0.82 pp | Avg. share of sample used (+0.82 pp effect) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed sample, one look | 5.2% | 54.1% | 100% |
| Naive peeking at 1.96 | **18.8%** | 66.8% | n/a (invalid) |
| O'Brien-Fleming | 5.6% | 51.6% | 87% |
| Pocock | 5.4% | 42.8% | 83% |
| mSPRT (tau 0.01) | 1.0% | 21.2% | 94% |

- The test suite checks five-look O'Brien-Fleming boundaries on 200,000 simulated normal null paths, allowing a 0.4 percentage-point tolerance around 5%. A separate test checks five-look Pocock boundaries within 0.05 of the textbook constant 2.413.
- The 5.6% OBF figure is a Monte Carlo estimate from 4,000 runs under the stated binomial setup, not a guarantee of exactly 5% error. The sample-use column describes the positive-effect scenario; under no effect, average sample use is about 99% for OBF, 97% for Pocock and 100% for mSPRT.
- The normal-mixture approximation was conservative in this simulation and paid in power. A plug-in variance estimate on binary outcomes does not establish an exact anytime-valid guarantee. A different prior width (tau) would shift that trade-off; I used one value and did not tune it.
- **Cookie Cats replay (illustration only).** The data has no timestamps, so I replayed the players in random order. Naive peeking would have declared a winner at look 4 (36,075 players, z = -2.51) and the z-score then drifted back toward 1.98. O'Brien-Fleming stops at look 8 (72,151 players, z = -2.94 vs bound 2.31). The normal-mixture running p-value estimate reaches 0.037 at the final look. Details: `reports/cookie_cats_sequential.json`.

## CUPED: same test, fewer players
`src/cuped.py` adjusts the outcome with a pre-experiment covariate. Simulation, 5,000 players per arm, true effect 0.30, 2,000 runs per row (`reports/cuped.json`):

| Correlation of covariate with outcome | Variance vs. unadjusted | Theory (1 - rho^2) | Mean estimate | 95% CI coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.300 | 96.0% |
| 0.3 | 0.91 | 0.91 | 0.300 | 95.8% |
| 0.5 | 0.75 | 0.75 | 0.300 | 94.7% |
| 0.7 | 0.51 | 0.51 | 0.300 | 95.7% |
| 0.9 | 0.19 | 0.19 | 0.300 | 94.0% |

Estimates stay unbiased and intervals keep their coverage. A covariate measured after assignment breaks this: when the treatment moves the covariate, CUPED returned 0.28 against a total effect of 0.55, a bias of -0.27. The covariate must be pre-experiment.

## Decisions and trade-offs
- **Alpha spending over a fixed look schedule.** Lan-DeMets spending functions let the number and timing of looks vary without breaking the error rate. `gs_boundaries` takes either a number of equally spaced looks or explicit information fractions such as `[0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1.0]`. Unequal spacing is checked by Monte Carlo (200,000 null paths, OBF and Pocock, within 0.4 points of 5%); the fractions must be planned in advance, and a data-dependent schedule is not covered.
- **O'Brien-Fleming as the default boundary.** It spends almost no alpha early, so an early look rarely stops the test on noise, and it ends near the fixed-sample cutoff (2.12 at look 10 vs 1.96). The price in the simulation is about 2.5 points of power against one look at the end (51.6% vs 54.1%). Pocock stops earlier on average but gave 42.8% power.
- **A solver written from scratch instead of a library.** I wrote the boundary solver so I could test it against the textbook Pocock constant and a five-look, 200,000-path normal-null simulation. Those regression checks are in the test suite; they do not establish validity for every outcome distribution or monitoring schedule.
- **mSPRT prior width (tau) not tuned.** One value, 0.01, chosen once. Tuning it to this simulation would flatter the method, so I did not.
- **Frequentist throughout.** The Bayesian check is a sanity number, not a sequential Bayesian design.

## Limits, stated plainly
- Public dataset, one game, one test. Players were randomly assigned by the game studio; I did not run the experiment.
- Cookie Cats has no pre-experiment metric, so CUPED is implemented and validated on simulated data only, not on this dataset.
- A split by rounds played is post-treatment (rounds can be affected by the gate), so the segment table in `reports/results.json` is descriptive, not causal.
- Simulations use idealized normal and binomial data; real metrics with heavy tails or novelty effects will behave less cleanly.
- Retention is binary. Revenue and lifetime value are not in the data.
- The SRM p-value of 0.0086 is a yellow flag, not proof of a broken split.

## Inference edge cases
Proportion tests validate integer counts and return an undefined relative lift (`None`) when the control conversion rate is zero. Two constant arms do not get a spurious zero-width Wald interval; CI fields are `None`, and sparse/boundary data need a separately chosen exact or score method. The CUPED mean comparison now uses Welch-Satterthwaite degrees of freedom and a Student-t interval, checked against [SciPy Welch inference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.ttest_ind.html). Misaligned covariates and undefined/constant-variance inference fail explicitly.

The CUPED simulation was regenerated with Welch t inference (seed 11, 2,000 runs for each of five correlations, 5,000 players per arm). The rounded table is unchanged; `reports/cuped.json` contains the refreshed values. The large-sample Cookie Cats proportion results are unaffected by these boundary guards.

The public planning, SRM, Holm, bootstrap, Bayesian and A/A helpers reject invalid counts, probabilities, arrays and simulation sizes before calculating a result. Normal-approximation planning requires interior baseline/treatment rates and target power strictly between 0.5 and 1. Sequential spending validates its information fractions and cumulative budget; negative/NaN likelihood ratios cannot produce negative p-values. Zero likelihood evidence maps to p=1 and infinite evidence to p=0, without division warnings.

Seeded invariant checks exercise arm-swap symmetry, Holm permutation/alpha monotonicity, bootstrap translation and spending-budget limits. These are numerical correctness checks, not new empirical evidence of Type-I error control. The historical reports and their stated inferential limitations remain unchanged.
